Clavicle and Scapula

Shoulder girdle = clavicle + scapula (pectoral girdle)

Clavicle: the big idea

  • The only bony attachment between the trunk and the upper limb
  • Palpable along its whole length
  • Gentle S-shape

Clavicle: two ends

Acromial (lateral) end

  • Flat
  • Small oval facet for the acromion of the scapula
  • Concave part, faces forward

Sternal (medial) end

  • Robust, somewhat quadrangular
  • Much larger facet: mainly manubrium of sternum, lesser extent first costal cartilage
  • Convex part, faces forward

Surfaces

  • Inferior surface, lateral third: conoid tubercle + trapezoid line, for the coracoclavicular ligament
  • Surfaces and margins are roughened by muscles linking clavicle to thorax, neck and upper limb
  • Superior surface is smoother than the inferior

Scapula: large, flat, triangular

Parts and what they do

Lateral angle

  • Shallow, comma-shaped glenoid cavity
  • Meets head of humerus: glenohumeral joint

Tubercles (round the glenoid)

  • Supraglenoid (above): long head of biceps brachii
  • Infraglenoid (below): long head of triceps brachii

Spine

  • Divides the posterior surface
  • Supraspinous fossa: small, superior
  • Infraspinous fossa: much larger, inferior

Acromion

  • Anterolateral projection of the spine
  • Arches over the glenohumeral joint
  • Small oval facet on its distal end meets the clavicle

Coracoid process

  • Hook-like, projects anterolaterally
  • Directly inferior to the lateral part of the clavicle

Notches

  • Suprascapular: small, just medial to the root of the coracoid, on the superior border
  • Greater scapular (spinoglenoid): between lateral angle and where the spine attaches

Palpable on a patient

  • Spine and acromion
  • Tip of the coracoid process
  • Inferior angle
  • Much of the medial border

Remember

  • Clavicle = only bony link: trunk to upper limb
  • Medial end convex, lateral end concave (both forward)
  • Conoid tubercle + trapezoid line → coracoclavicular ligament
  • Scapula: 3 · 3 · 2 · 3 (angles, borders, surfaces, processes)
  • Supra = biceps (above), Infra = triceps (below)
